## 2026-10-27 - SQLite Optimization Pattern: batch inserts and `last_insert_rowid()`
**Learning:** Python's `sqlite3` driver sets `cur.lastrowid` to `None` after `executemany()`. To retrieve auto-incremented IDs for batch inserts without a `RETURNING` clause (for compatibility or simpler queries), execute `SELECT last_insert_rowid()` immediately after the batch insert. Since rows inserted in a single transaction with `AUTOINCREMENT` are assigned contiguous IDs, you can calculate the preceding inserted IDs backward using `cur.rowcount` or `len(batch)`. Also, to ensure strict uniqueness for timestamps (a regression constraint) during batch operations while keeping the speed of `executemany()`, applying a minor offset (`time.time() + (i * 1e-6)`) is an effective and safe approach.
**Action:** When migrating loops of `execute()` to `executemany()` for performance improvements in SQLite, apply this pattern to correctly retrieve auto-incremented IDs and ensure constraints like strictly unique timestamps are maintained.
